1. Introduction
--------------

1.1 Background
Low-Density Parity-Check (LDPC) codes have emerged as one of the most powerful error-correcting codes in modern communication systems. Their near-capacity performance and efficient implementation have made them the standard choice for various applications, from wireless communications to data storage. However, traditional LDPC decoding algorithms face challenges in terms of complexity, convergence speed, and performance in low Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) regions.

1.2 Problem Statement
The key challenges in LDPC decoding include:
- Slow convergence in traditional message-passing algorithms
- Performance degradation in low SNR conditions
- High computational complexity for large codes
- Limited adaptability to different channel conditions
- Memory-intensive implementations for long codes

1.3 Project Goals
This thesis aims to address these challenges through:
- Development of a neural network-based LDPC decoder
- Implementation of efficient weight sharing mechanisms
- Integration of residual connections for improved training
- Optimization of memory usage and computational efficiency
- Achievement of better error correction performance
